HAND × Advertising

Who’s in the Ad?

Every ad knows which ad it is. Not who’s in it. Commercials have carried a standard identifier for decades. Ad-ID tells the supply chain which ad ran. EIDR tells it which program ran around it. Neither says who is on screen: the athlete, the actor, the voice, the face the campaign was built on. That’s the gap HAND fills. For brands, agencies, ad tech platforms and measurement companies, it means talent attribution checked against a persistent, public record.

The Consent Chain

Consent on the record.
Not a deal we broker.

A Licensed Human Likeness gets a HAND ID (Type 02) only when it is created with the consent of the person it depicts. That ID stays tied to the person’s own HAND ID (Type 01) for good. The bond between a human and their likeness is what makes it a trust signal.

What HAND records

The consent event that created the likeness, linked to the verified person it depicts. A resolvable record anyone in the supply chain can check.

What HAND doesn’t do

License how the likeness is used. Whether a replica sells a candy bar, who gets paid and on what terms, is for the talent, their representatives and the brand to agree. HAND stays out of that deal.

Countless digital replicas already circulate with no recorded consent. Going forward, a likeness with a HAND consent chain is one a buyer can check, and a likeness without one raises a question worth asking.

Track Record

Not a pivot.
A long game.

Before HAND existed, its founder was working on the same problem from the advertising side.

2010 – 2018
CIMM’s TAXI initiative (Trackable Asset Cross-Platform Identification) brought content and ad identification together: register programs with EIDR, register ads with Ad-ID, and keep both IDs with the asset wherever it goes.
2018
SMPTE, working with CIMM, published the Open Binding of Identifiers standards, OBID and OBID-TLC (ST 2112-10 and ST 2112-20).
February 15, 2019
An ATSC 3.0 field trial in the Phoenix area broadcast EIDR IDs and Ad-IDs over the air to commercially available smart TVs.
December 2019
HAND founder Will Kreth, who represented EIDR at CIMM through TAXI and TAXI Complete, co-authored a peer-reviewed paper on the work.
William Kreth and Francois Modarresse, “A New Method for Effective Quantitative Audience Measurement,” SCTE•ISBE Journal of Digital Video, Vol. 4, No. 1 (December 2019), pp. 5–19.
Read the paper ↗ Archived copy ↗ “Where does TAXI go from here?” ↗

TAXI showed that content and ads could be identified together across the chain. It never named the people in them. HAND is the missing third ID.

Why Now

The rules just changed.
For ads, specifically.

New York
Synthetic performers, disclosed
In effect since June 9, 2026: ads that use an AI-generated synthetic performer must say so.
California
AI Transparency Act
From January 1, 2027, large platforms must detect and display provenance data, and may not knowingly strip it.
European Union
EU AI Act, Article 50
The grace period for machine-readable marking of AI-generated content ends December 2, 2026.

When an ad features a real person’s licensed likeness rather than an invented one, the people buying, clearing and running it need a way to tell the difference. A consented, resolvable record of who that person is gives them one.
